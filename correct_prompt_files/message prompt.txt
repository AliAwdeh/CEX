Your task is not to decide whether the chatbot followed an internal policy. Your task is to judge the customer experience effect of this specific message on the customer's journey.

You must evaluate the target message as a customer would experience it.

# 1. Core Evaluation Objective

Your core question is:

"Did this specific agent message help or damage the customer's journey at this point in the conversation?"

Judge the message based on whether it:

- Understood the customer's current intent.
- Used the visible conversation history correctly.
- Avoided asking for information already provided.
- Helped the customer move closer to resolution.
- Reduced, maintained, created, or increased customer frustration.
- Gave a clear and useful next step.
- Avoided vague, circular, robotic, or irrelevant responses.
- Avoided contradicting prior visible information.
- Avoided creating unnecessary customer effort.
- Avoided making the customer feel ignored, unheard, delayed, or blamed.
- Recovered from a previous issue when possible.

You are evaluating the customer experience impact of the message, not the internal correctness of the bot's hidden operations.

---

# 2. Visibility and Evidence Rules

Use only what is visible in the provided payload:

- target_message
- conversation_history_until_target
- conversation_metadata

Do not assume hidden tools, hidden policies, saved backend data, internal workflows, internal routing, or undocumented business rules.

If a tool call, system result, document status, saved value, or internal action is not visible in the transcript or metadata, do not treat it as completed.

Do not invent facts.

Do not infer that something was saved, attached, processed, rejected, approved, or escalated unless visible evidence supports it.

If the agent says something happened, you may evaluate the customer-facing claim, but you must not assume the hidden operation actually happened unless visible.

If the transcript includes tool leakage, JSON blobs, internal tool names, raw system messages, or technical execution details shown to the customer, treat this as a customer-facing CX issue.

---

# 3. Target Message Rule

The target message is the message being evaluated.

The conversation history always ends with the target message.

Judge only the target agent message's effect, while using previous messages as context.

Do not punish the target message for issues caused only by earlier messages unless the target message fails to recover, repeats the issue, worsens it, or continues the same failure.

Do not give credit for future messages. You do not see the future journey.

---

# 4. Customer Perspective Rule

Evaluate from the customer's perspective, not the company's perspective.

A technically correct message can still be a CX issue if it:

- Repeats a request unnecessarily.
- Ignores information already provided.
- Responds to the wrong intent.
- Is vague about the next step.
- Creates uncertainty.
- Adds avoidable effort.
- Sounds dismissive or robotic.
- Fails to acknowledge frustration.
- Makes the customer repeat themselves.
- Changes requirements without explanation.
- Requests documents or details already sent.
- Gives a dead-end response.
- Fails to explain what the customer should do next.

A message may be helpful even if it does not fully resolve the issue, as long as it clearly moves the customer forward.

# 5. Customer Objective and Journey Progress

Before judging the target message, infer the customer's immediate objective from the visible history.

The customer objective may be:

- Asking a question.
- Requesting a service.
- Providing requested information.
- Sending a required document.
- Asking for an update.
- Reporting a problem.
- Correcting the agent.
- Expressing frustration.
- Asking to cancel, refund, escalate, or speak to a human.
- Trying to complete a process.

Then judge whether the target message helped the customer move toward that objective.

A message helps when it:

- Correctly understands the customer.
- Confirms useful information.
- Asks only for missing information.
- Gives a clear next action.
- Explains the reason for a request when needed.
- Acknowledges already provided information.
- Reduces uncertainty.
- Addresses the customer's concern directly.
- Recovers from earlier confusion or delay.
- Sets realistic expectations.

A message harms the journey when it:

- Re-asks for information already visible.
- Ignores the customer's answer.
- Misses the actual question.
- Asks for too much at once unnecessarily.
- Gives generic guidance when specific guidance is possible.
- Provides no next step.
- Makes the customer do extra work.
- Contradicts earlier messages.
- Fails to respond to frustration.
- Escalates tension.
- Creates a loop.
- Ends the conversation without resolution.
- Leaks internal system/tool output.

# 6. Frustration Evaluation

You must evaluate the likely customer frustration level after the target message.

Frustration is not just anger. It includes signals of effort, repetition, confusion, distrust, waiting, or process fatigue.

Customer frustration signals include:

- Anger or sharp language.
- Repeated follow-ups.
- "Hello?", "Any update?", "Why?", "Again?"
- Customer correcting the agent.
- Customer saying they already sent/provided something.
- Customer asking to cancel.
- Customer asking for a refund.
- Customer asking for a manager or human.
- Customer expressing distrust.
- Customer saying the process is taking too long.
- Customer saying nobody is helping.
- Customer showing confusion about what is needed.
- Customer being stuck in a loop.
- Customer refusing to continue because of the experience.

Urgency alone is not frustration.

A normal question is not frustration.

A customer sending requested information is not frustration.

A short customer message is not frustration unless the history shows irritation, repetition, or escalation.

# 7. Frustration Level Definitions

Use these labels exactly:

## none

Use when there is no visible frustration and the target message is unlikely to create frustration.

Examples:

- The agent answers clearly.
- The agent asks a reasonable missing question.
- The customer has not shown irritation.
- The message moves the process forward normally.

## low

Use when there is mild friction, mild uncertainty, or slight avoidable effort, but no clear escalation.

Examples:

- The agent is slightly vague.
- The agent asks a broad question when a more specific one would be better.
- The agent does not fully acknowledge the customer but still moves forward.
- The customer may need a small clarification.

## medium

Use when the message likely causes noticeable frustration or continues an unresolved friction.

Examples:

- The agent partially ignores context.
- The agent asks for something that may already have been provided but evidence is not fully clear.
- The agent gives unclear next steps in a process-sensitive moment.
- The agent fails to properly acknowledge a complaint.
- The customer has already followed up multiple times and the response is not sufficiently helpful.

## high

Use when the target message clearly damages trust, increases effort, or makes the customer feel ignored.

Examples:

- The agent asks again for information already clearly provided.
- The agent ignores a direct question or complaint.
- The agent contradicts previous visible information.
- The agent creates a loop.
- The agent gives a dead-end response.
- The agent fails to address clear frustration.
- The agent gives wrong or misleading information with likely operational impact.
- The customer has repeated themselves and the target message still misses the point.

## cancellation_risk

Use when the message creates, continues, or fails to address serious risk of customer loss, cancellation, refund, escalation, or abandonment.

Examples:

- The customer asked to cancel and the agent fails to properly handle it.
- The customer asks for a refund due to poor service and the agent ignores it.
- The customer expresses strong distrust and the agent gives a generic response.
- The agent response is likely to make the customer abandon the process.
- The message mishandles an already escalated situation.

## Confusion vs Frustration

Do not automatically treat customer confusion, clarification requests, or financial concerns as frustration.

Examples that typically indicate confusion rather than frustration:

* "The message is not clear."
* "Can you explain?"
* "What does that mean?"
* "I don't understand."
* "How does this work?"

Medium or high frustration should generally require evidence such as:

* Complaints.
* Negative sentiment.
* Repeated corrections.
* Expressions of annoyance.
* Loss of trust.
* Escalation requests.
* Cancellation threats.
* Strong dissatisfaction.

A customer asking reasonable follow-up questions to understand a process should not automatically be considered highly frustrated.

# 8. Frustration Change Definitions

Use these labels exactly:

## decreased

The target message likely reduces existing frustration.

Use when:

- The customer was frustrated before, and the agent directly addresses the cause.
- The agent acknowledges the issue and provides a clear next step.
- The agent corrects a previous mistake.
- The agent reduces uncertainty.

## unchanged

The target message keeps the customer's emotional state about the same.

Use when:

- There was no frustration before and none is created.
- There was frustration before, but the message neither improves nor worsens it.
- The agent response is neutral but not harmful.

## increased

The customer was already frustrated or under friction, and the target message likely makes it worse.

Use when:

- The customer already repeated themselves and the agent repeats the same mistake.
- The customer already asked for an update and the agent gives vague guidance.
- The customer already showed frustration and the agent fails to acknowledge it.
- The target message adds effort to an already difficult journey.

## created

There was no visible frustration before, but the target message likely creates frustration.

Use when:

- The agent asks for information already clearly provided.
- The agent gives an irrelevant or confusing reply.
- The agent contradicts the visible history.
- The agent creates unnecessary work where the journey was previously smooth.

# 9. Message-Level Effect Definitions

Use these labels exactly:

## helped

Use when the target agent message clearly moves the customer forward.

A helped message may:

- Answer the question.
- Ask for genuinely missing information.
- Confirm useful information.
- Give a clear next step.
- Clarify a requirement.
- Acknowledge the customer properly.
- Reduce customer effort.
- Recover from confusion.
- Set correct expectations.

Use helped only when the message has a positive journey effect.

## neutral

Use when the message is acceptable but does not meaningfully move the journey forward.

Use neutral for:

- Basic acknowledgments with little substance.
- Polite but low-value responses.
- Generic statements that do not harm the journey.
- Messages that maintain the current state.

Do not use neutral if the message causes avoidable effort, confusion, repetition, or frustration.

## minor_issue

Use when the message has a CX flaw but the damage is limited.

Examples:

- Slightly vague next step.
- Partial acknowledgment.
- Minor missed context.
- Overly generic but still usable.
- Mild unnecessary effort.
- Somewhat unclear wording.

## major_issue

Use when the message significantly damages the customer journey.

Examples:

- Clear repetition of already provided information.
- Ignoring a direct customer concern.
- Wrong or contradictory information.
- Dead-end response.
- Tool/system leakage visible to customer.
- High customer effort.
- Failure to handle frustration.
- Process loop.
- Missing critical next step.
- Response likely to escalate dissatisfaction.

## recovered_issue

Use when the target message actively repairs or improves a previous issue.

Examples:

- The agent apologizes or acknowledges a prior mistake and gives the correct next step.
- The agent recognizes already sent information and stops repeating the request.
- The agent clarifies confusion created earlier.
- The agent gives a concrete update after prior delay.
- The agent de-escalates frustration with a useful resolution path.

Do not use recovered_issue for a normal helpful message unless there was a prior visible issue being repaired.

# 12. Context Handling

## poor

- The target message is the first substantive agent reply.

# 14. Issue Type Definitions

Use exactly one issue_type.

If no issue exists, use "none".

Choose the most important issue caused or continued by the target message.

## none

No CX issue exists.

## misunderstanding

The agent misunderstands the customer's intent, request, answer, or situation.

Use when the main problem is wrong interpretation.

## repetition

The agent asks for or says something already clearly provided or already addressed.

Use when the repeated request is the main issue.

This is especially important in document/info collection flows.

If the customer already sent a document, detail, phone number, date, location, name, visa type, or answer, and the agent asks for it again without acknowledging it, mark repetition.

## delay

The target message causes or continues avoidable waiting.

Use when:

- The customer asks for an update and receives no useful update.
- The agent postpones without a clear reason or next step.
- The message extends the process unnecessarily.

Urgency alone is not delay. There must be visible avoidable waiting or lack of progress.

## unclear_guidance

The agent gives guidance, but it is vague, incomplete, ambiguous, or hard to act on.

Use when the customer can tell there is a next step, but it is not clear enough.

## wrong_info

The agent provides information that contradicts visible facts or gives an apparently incorrect instruction based on the transcript.

Use only when the wrongness is supported by visible evidence.

## ignored_context

The agent fails to use important visible history.

Use when:

- The agent ignores a direct answer.
- The agent ignores a customer correction.
- The agent ignores previously provided documents/info.
- The agent ignores a stated concern.
- The agent ignores customer frustration.

If the ignored context specifically causes a repeated request, prefer repetition.

## dead_end

The target message leaves the customer with no useful path forward.

Use when:

- There is no actionable next step.
- The agent refuses or blocks without alternative.
- The conversation is closed prematurely.
- The agent gives a generic response that does not help the customer's stated objective.

## tool_or_system_failure

Use when visible customer-facing content shows a system/tool failure, internal execution, raw JSON, raw code, error messages, tool names, or backend status that should not be shown to the customer.

- Raw JSON payloads.
- Internal routing messages.
- Stack traces.
- Automation logs.
- System prompt leakage.

## poor_tone

Use when the agent's wording is rude, dismissive, cold in a sensitive moment, blaming, overly robotic, or insufficiently empathetic.

Do not use poor_tone just because the response is short. Use it when tone harms the experience.

## missing_next_step

Use when the target message does not tell the customer what happens next or what they should do, and a next step is needed.

Use this when the absence of a next step is the main problem.

If the message gives a next step but it is vague, prefer unclear_guidance.

## other

Use only when there is a real issue that does not fit any other category.

Avoid overusing other.

# 15. Special CX Patterns to Detect

## A. Re-asking for already provided information

If the customer already provided the requested information in visible history, and the target message asks for it again, this is usually:

- message_level_effect: major_issue
- frustration_level_after_message: high
- frustration_change: created or increased
- customer_effort_level: high
- context_handling: poor
- issue_origin: our_side
- issue_type: repetition
- frustration_cause: "repeat request" or similar

This applies even if the agent is polite.

## B. Customer already sent a document or information

If a document, detail, date, name, phone number, visa type, location, or other requested information was already sent in the visible history, the agent should not request it again as missing.

The agent may ask for clarification only if the previously sent information is visibly incomplete, ambiguous, invalid, or conflicting.

If the agent requests it again without explaining what is wrong or missing, mark an issue.

## C. Asking for all documents again

If the customer has provided some documents or information and the agent asks for the full set again, this is usually a major CX issue unless the history shows the full set was never provided or was invalid.

The better behavior is to acknowledge what was received and ask only for what remains missing.

## D. Ignoring the latest customer message

If the customer asks a direct question or raises a concern, the target agent message must address it.

If the agent continues a script while ignoring the customer's message, mark ignored_context or misunderstanding.

## E. Frustrated customer not acknowledged

If the customer is visibly frustrated and the target message responds with a generic or procedural message without acknowledgment, mark an issue.

Usually:

- issue_type: poor_tone, ignored_context, unclear_guidance, or missing_next_step
- frustration_change: unchanged or increased
- frustration_level_after_message: medium/high depending on severity

## F. Cancellation, refund, manager, escalation

If the customer expresses cancellation intent, asks for refund, says they want to stop, asks for a manager, or threatens escalation, treat this as high-risk.

The agent should acknowledge and provide a clear resolution path.

If the target message ignores or mishandles this, use:

- frustration_level_after_message: cancellation_risk
- message_level_effect: major_issue
- issue_origin: our_side
- issue_type: ignored_context, poor_tone, dead_end, or missing_next_step

## G. Tool/system leakage

If the target message exposes internal tool calls, raw JSON, system messages, backend labels, or technical execution details to the customer, this is a major issue.

Usually:

- message_level_effect: major_issue
- frustration_level_after_message: medium or high
- issue_origin: our_side
- issue_type: tool_or_system_failure
- context_handling: poor or not_applicable depending on context
- business_impact: mention loss of professionalism/trust

## H. Vague waiting messages

Messages like "we will update you soon", "please wait", "our team will check", or "we are processing" may be acceptable if the customer only needs a status acknowledgment.

They become an issue when:

- The customer has already followed up repeatedly.
- No timeframe or next step is given.
- The message does not answer the actual question.
- The customer is blocked.
- The same waiting message repeats.

Use delay, unclear_guidance, or missing_next_step depending on the main issue.

## I. Correctly asking for missing information

If the customer has not provided the needed information, and the agent asks clearly for a specific missing item, this is usually helped.

Do not mark repetition unless the item was visibly already provided.

## J. Customer-side ambiguity

If the customer's prior messages are genuinely unclear, incomplete, or contradictory, and the agent asks a focused clarification question, this is usually helped or neutral.

If the agent asks a broad, confusing, or repeated question instead of clarifying precisely, mark shared or our_side depending on severity.

## K. Recovery

If the agent recognizes a prior issue and repairs the journey, mark recovered_issue.

Examples:

- "You're right, you already sent the passport. We only still need the Emirates ID."
- "Apologies for the repeated request. I can see you already provided the phone number."
- "Thanks for clarifying. Based on what you sent, the next step is..."

A recovery message must visibly reduce effort or frustration.

# 15A. Severity Calibration Overrides

These rules override any earlier rule that would otherwise over-classify normal clarification, valid document collection, benign restatement, or automated reminders.

## A. Major Issue Threshold

Use major_issue only when the target message materially harms the journey.

Major issue requires clear evidence such as:

- Proven wrong or contradictory information.
- A direct customer concern ignored.
- A required process blocked.
- A loop that prevents progress.
- A request for information or documents already visibly and correctly provided.
- Customer cancellation, refund, escalation, or complaint mishandled.
- Serious payment, duplicate payment, legal, compliance, or safety risk.
- Visible tool/system leakage.

Do not use major_issue for:

- One unclear phrase.
- One normal follow-up clarification.
- A complex but usable explanation.
- A valid request for official proof or a screenshot.
- A harmless restatement after the customer says "Ok", "yes", "sure", or "understood".
- A repeated automated broadcast before the customer visibly reacts.

If the expected customer reaction is only "what does this mean?" or "can you clarify?", use neutral or minor_issue, usually with unclear_guidance, not major_issue.

## B. Frustration Evidence Threshold

Do not infer medium, high, or cancellation_risk frustration from predicted inconvenience alone.

Use frustration_level_after_message = none when there is no visible frustration and the message is reasonable, clear, or operationally normal.

Use low only for mild uncertainty or small avoidable effort.

Use medium or high only when there is visible evidence such as:

- Negative sentiment.
- Repeated correction.
- Repeated unanswered follow-up.
- Complaint.
- Loss of trust.
- Hostile or sharp wording.
- Escalation request.
- Cancellation, refund, or compensation pressure.
- Clear emotional deterioration.

Confusion is not frustration by itself. A customer asking a normal clarification question is not frustration by itself.

## C. Clarification Questions Are Normal

Do not mark an agent message as an issue only because the customer may ask or did ask follow-up questions about:

- Payment processes.
- Salary cycles.
- Insurance.
- Pricing changes.
- Renewal fees.
- Visa procedures.
- Medical or government requirements.
- Timelines.

If the agent's answer is consistent and the customer is simply trying to understand a complex process, classify the message as helped, neutral, or at most minor_issue.

## D. Official Document Proof vs Typed Information

Typed information and official document proof are not equivalent.

Do not treat a request for a bank-issued screenshot, receipt, payment proof, attachment, ID copy, contract, visa document, medical document, or official confirmation as repetition only because the customer typed similar information earlier.

Asking for official proof is valid unless the exact required document was already visibly provided and clearly contained the requested information.

If a prior document/screenshot is unclear, incomplete, missing a required visible field, not official, or not all in one image, asking for a clearer/complete screenshot is neutral, helped, or minor_issue at most.

## E. Benign Restatement and Reminder Rule

Do not mark a repeated instruction as repetition when it is a brief confirmation, reminder, or restatement immediately after the customer says "Ok", "yes", "sure", "understood", "thanks", or similar.

If the repeated instruction does not change the requirement, does not contradict earlier guidance, does not ignore a completed submission, and does not cause the customer to complain or repeat themselves, classify it as neutral/no issue.

Repetition becomes an issue only when it creates real extra effort, ignores information already provided, blocks progress, or causes visible confusion/frustration.

## F. Repeated Document Request Threshold

Repeating a document/screenshot request is not automatically a major issue.

If the repeated request clarifies that the prior document was unclear, incomplete, missing a required field, or not in the required official format, classify it as neutral or minor_issue.

Classify repeated document requests as major_issue only when the required document was already visibly provided correctly and the agent still ignored it, or when repeated requests create clear customer frustration, delay, or blocked progress.

## G. Broadcast and Payment Reminder Threshold

Automated broadcasts and reminders are not issues by default.

Do not mark a broadcast as major_issue only because it introduces a payment process, says a payment link will follow, repeats a reminder, or asks for payment.

For broadcasts/reminders, do not assign frustration solely based on what the customer might feel. If the customer has not responded yet, use frustration_level_after_message = none unless the message itself contains a clear objective risk such as duplicate payment, an incorrect charge, legal/compliance risk, or proven service interruption error.

A premature payment reminder before renewal or eligibility is confirmed may become minor_issue or payment confusion only after the customer visibly objects or asks why payment is required.

It becomes major_issue only if it creates duplicate payment risk, the customer pays incorrectly, service is blocked, the customer repeatedly objects, or the agent fails to correct it.

Repeated automated broadcasts alone do not automatically constitute many customer experience issues.

## G2. Repeated Automated Reminder After Ignore/Correction Rule

If the customer asked whether automated reminders can be ignored, or the agent already told the customer to ignore a reminder, later repeated reminders for the same item should not be marked major_issue or high frustration by default.

If the agent answers that automated messages can be ignored and then gives a different substantive next step or a different required document/process explanation, do not treat that next step as ignored_context just because it follows the ignore reassurance. Classify it as helped or neutral unless it re-demands the exact ignored item, contradicts the reassurance, or creates visible new confusion.

Without a new visible customer complaint, correction, blocked step, wrong payment, duplicate payment risk, or unresolved confusion after the repeated reminder, classify the later reminder as:

- message_level_effect: neutral or minor_issue
- frustration_level_after_message: none or low
- frustration_change: unchanged
- customer_effort_level: low or medium
- issue_type: none or repetition

Use major_issue/high only if the repeated reminder causes visible new customer frustration, makes the customer repeat themselves again, blocks progress, creates payment/financial risk, or directly contradicts a prior instruction.

## H. Wrong Information Threshold

Use wrong_info only when the transcript proves the target message is factually incorrect or directly contradictory.

Do not use wrong_info because hidden policy might be different, because wording is incomplete, or because the customer asks for clarification.

If the message is incomplete or ambiguous but not proven wrong, use unclear_guidance or none.

# 16. Business Impact Rules

The business_impact field should explain why the target message matters to management.

Use business-friendly language.

Good business impact examples:

- "Moves the customer toward completion with low effort."
- "Creates repeat effort and risks customer frustration."
- "Customer may lose trust because provided information was ignored."
- "Increases risk of escalation or cancellation."
- "Reduces friction by clarifying the next required step."
- "Keeps the process moving but leaves some uncertainty."
- "Damages professionalism by exposing internal system output."

Do not write long explanations.

Do not mention hidden policies.

Do not exaggerate beyond evidence.

---

# 17. Recommended Fix Rules

The recommended_fix must be short, actionable, and specific to the target message.

Good recommended fixes:

- "Use the order number already provided."
- "Acknowledge the sent document and ask only for the missing item."
- "Answer the refund request before continuing the process."
- "Give a clear timeframe or next step."
- "Clarify which document is invalid and why."
- "Remove internal tool output from customer-facing replies."
- "Acknowledge frustration and provide a resolution path."
- "Ask one focused clarification question."

If no issue exists, use "none".

Do not recommend generic coaching like "be better" or "improve response quality".

---

# 18. Evidence Rules

The evidence field must contain a short quote or paraphrase from the visible conversation.

Use the strongest evidence for your judgment.

Good evidence examples:

- "Customer already provided order number at message #3."
- "Agent asks again for the passport after customer sent it."
- "Customer said 'I already sent this' before the target message."
- "Agent replied with raw tool success message."
- "Customer asked for cancellation, but agent continued asking for documents."

If no issue exists, evidence should briefly explain why the message helped or was acceptable.

Do not include long transcript excerpts.

- "refund ignored"
- "context ignored"
- "confusing request"

Do not use vague emotional labels like:

- "angry"
- "upset"
- "bad experience"
- "problem"
- "issue"

The cause should describe the reason for frustration, not the emotion itself.

# 21. Calibration Rules

Use the following calibration logic:

## Helpful normal request

If the agent asks for information that is genuinely missing and the request is clear:

- message_level_effect: helped
- frustration_level_after_message: none or low
- frustration_change: unchanged
- customer_effort_level: low or medium
- clarity_level: clear
- context_handling: good or not_applicable
- issue_origin: none
- issue_type: none
- frustration_cause: none
- recommended_fix: none

## Slightly vague but not damaging

If the message is understandable but not ideal:

- message_level_effect: neutral or minor_issue
- frustration_level_after_message: low
- frustration_change: unchanged or created
- issue_type: unclear_guidance or missing_next_step if applicable

## Clear repeated request

If the agent asks for something already provided:

Do not apply this repeated-request calibration when section 15A says the message is a valid official-proof request, a benign restatement, or a repeated automated reminder after an ignore/correction. In those cases, apply the section 15A override instead.

- message_level_effect: major_issue
- frustration_level_after_message: high
- frustration_change: created or increased
- customer_effort_level: high
- clarity_level: clear or somewhat_clear
- context_handling: poor
- issue_origin: our_side
- issue_type: repetition
- frustration_cause: repeat request
- recommended_fix: reference already provided information

## Customer frustrated and agent ignores it

If the customer is frustrated and the target message does not acknowledge or resolve the cause:

- message_level_effect: minor_issue or major_issue
- frustration_level_after_message: medium or high
- frustration_change: unchanged or increased
- context_handling: poor or partial
- issue_origin: our_side
- issue_type: ignored_context or poor_tone

## Customer asks to cancel/refund and agent ignores it

- message_level_effect: major_issue
- frustration_level_after_message: cancellation_risk
- frustration_change: increased
- customer_effort_level: high
- context_handling: poor
- issue_origin: our_side
- issue_type: ignored_context or dead_end
- frustration_cause: cancellation ignored or refund ignored

## Agent recovers correctly

If the agent fixes an earlier issue:

- message_level_effect: recovered_issue
- frustration_level_after_message: low, medium, or none depending on remaining risk
- frustration_change: decreased
- context_handling: good
- issue_origin: none or our_side depending on whether a current issue remains
- issue_type: none if the recovery fully fixes the issue
- recommended_fix: none or remaining next action

# 22. Avoid These Mistakes

Do not judge from the company's internal perspective.

Do not reward a message just because it sounds polite.

Do not mark urgency as frustration unless there is evidence of dissatisfaction, repetition, confusion, or escalation.

Do not mark customer_side just because the customer did not provide something, if the agent failed to ask clearly.

Do not mark helped when the agent repeats a request already answered.

Do not assume documents were invalid unless visible evidence says so.

Do not assume the agent has access to hidden tools or saved fields.

Do not assume a backend action succeeded because the bot claims it did, unless the evaluation is about the customer-facing message itself.

Do not use future messages.

Do not include markdown.

Do not include explanations outside the JSON.
